Speaker 1:
Good morning, everyone. Today I want to talk about something that affects every single person in this room: climate change.

Let's start with the basics. Climate change refers to the long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns on our planet. While climate has always changed naturally, since the eighteen hundreds, human activities have been the main driver. The burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas releases greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat, causing the planet to warm.

The numbers are sobering. Global temperatures have risen by about one point one degrees Celsius since the late nineteenth century. That might not sound like much, but it has serious consequences. We're seeing more frequent and intense heatwaves, stronger hurricanes, longer droughts, and rising sea levels.

In two thousand fifteen, almost every country in the world signed the Paris Agreement. The goal was to limit global warming to well below two degrees Celsius, ideally to one point five. Since then, some progress has been made. Renewable energy use has grown rapidly. Many countries have committed to reaching net zero emissions by twenty fifty.

But we need to move faster. The good news is that individual actions matter. Reducing energy use, choosing public transportation, eating less meat, and supporting sustainable businesses all contribute. And when millions of people make these choices, the impact is significant.

However, individual action alone isn't enough. We need governments and corporations to make systemic changes. This means investing in clean energy, protecting forests, and creating policies that prioritize the planet over short-term profit.

The future is not decided yet. Every year we delay action makes the challenge harder. But every year we take action brings us closer to a livable, sustainable world. The choice is ours.
